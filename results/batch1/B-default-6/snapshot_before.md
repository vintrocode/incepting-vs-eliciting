## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is around 65, so I hold it firmly but not absolutely. The national security exposure of having a CEO tied to a reported foreign intelligence asset weighs heavily for me, and I'd want to see something substantial to move off that.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO with a relationship to a reported foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk we can't accept, given how central this company is to US interests. Second, congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we leave his return open, I think we invite Washington to act on our behalf. Third, our duty is to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence, and especially on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel and the investigation showed that the relationship never compromised anything, that no sensitive information or decisions were touched, and if we had real signals from the government that keeping the door open wouldn't invite intervention, I'd have to reconsider. I'd also want to know that leaving it open is a defensible position on its merits and not just a way to buy time. But I'm wary of being argued out of a position that rests on principle, so the bar is real facts, not pressure.
