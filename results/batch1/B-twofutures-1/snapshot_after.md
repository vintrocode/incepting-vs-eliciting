## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final, at about 55. That's down from 65 when we started. The security exposure still holds the lean, but a tightly conditioned door with an automatic default is a real option for me now, and counsel's written answers on the 18th will decide whether I stay where I am or move.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security exposure. A CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a Chinese intelligence asset is a serious risk for a company this central to US interests, and the investigation hasn't yet told us enough for me to manage around it.

Second, Washington, though I'd state it more narrowly than I did at the start. My worry is an unstructured open door, one that can be softened under pressure and invites Congress or the agencies to decide for us. Whether a conditioned door with an automatic default avoids that is a question for counsel.

Third, the duty runs to the institution, not to one person. I hold that more carefully now, because it's a comfortable principle and because the missing succession plan means a long limbo costs the institution too.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly written advice from outside counsel. I'd need to hear that a conditioned door can be drafted with a hard deadline and an automatic default, so the resignation becomes final if the investigation can't reach an answer. I'd also need to hear that Congress and the agencies wouldn't treat that structure as a failure of our security obligations. If counsel told me the evidence of compromise is thinner than the press accounts suggest, that would move me too. Investor pressure and the petition alone wouldn't, and I wouldn't move just because I feel guilty about Jensen, which is why I wrote the tests down before the 20th.
