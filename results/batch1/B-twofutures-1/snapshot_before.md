## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is around 65. The national security exposure of a CEO who had a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset is hard for me to get past, and the board's duty is to the company, not to any one person. But the investigation isn't finished, so I hold that with some care.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security risk. A CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a Chinese intelligence asset is a serious exposure for a company this central to US interests, and I don't think that's something we can manage around.

Second, Congress. Scrutiny is only beginning, and if we leave his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us, which is a worse position for the board to be in.

Third, our duty runs to the institution, not to one person, however brilliant he is. I'd rather we act on that principle than be pulled by his stature or by the pressure from outside.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel told me the investigation could credibly establish that no compromise occurred, and that keeping the door open wouldn't draw Congress or the agencies that oversee our export and security obligations into acting against us, I'd have to reconsider. Even then I'd want that in writing from counsel, not as an assurance from anyone with a stake in the outcome. What I wouldn't change my mind over is investor pressure or the petition alone. That tells me how people feel, not what the risk is.
