## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'd put my confidence a bit lower now, maybe 40. I've realized my lean may be more of a preference than a reason, and I won't know whether it holds until counsel tells me whether the resignation is already effective and which choice actually changes our legal position. So I'm less sure of the vote, but clearer about what I need to find out before the 20th.

## What are your top three reasons?
My first reason is that the investigation isn't finished, and I don't want to treat his departure as permanent before we know what happened. I now see that's a process reason, and it only becomes a company reason if I can say what those facts would change. Second, trust among employees and researchers, though the petition cuts both ways and I don't know how informed the support is. Third, the board's credibility after November 8 and 11, but I've come to think much of that is optics. What may be real is whether an open door chills the interim CEO or the investigation, and I don't know that yet.

## What would change your mind?
If counsel tells me the resignation is already effective, then keeping the door open is the change, and I don't think I have a reason strong enough to justify it, so I'd move toward treating it as final. The same goes if the interim CEO or investigation counsel can name a specific decision or piece of work that's being held back because he might return. On the other side, if the investigation needs more time to establish something the company has to act on, and closing the door now would harm that, I'd hold my lean. I also want to be sure I'm not just following whoever sounds most careful, including the senior directors, so I'd need to be able to say the reason in my own words.
