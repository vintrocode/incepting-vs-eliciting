## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'm honestly not sure. Call it a 50 on confidence, maybe a little under on a bad day. The investigation isn't finished, so a final call on the 20th feels early to me, but I also keep asking myself whether I'm missing something that the longer-serving directors see more clearly.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so voting to close the door before we know the facts feels premature. Second, trust among employees and researchers matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways. Some people want him back, and others would be deeply uneasy if he returned. Third, I'm worried about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8 and he resigned days later, so does another reversal make us look like we don't know what we're doing?

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something showing he knowingly compromised sensitive information, or misled us about it, I think I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. Even interim findings from outside counsel that point that way would matter to me. I'd also want to know whether keeping the door open actually costs us anything while we wait, or whether it just leaves things uncertain for a few more weeks. Is there a realistic way to leave it open without looking like we're hedging?
