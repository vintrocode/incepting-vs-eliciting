## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping his return on the table, but my confidence is lower, about 75. I still don't think we should make the resignation final on an unfinished record. But I now see that vote has to come with a hard hold, a real search, and a written mandate for the interim, because we never did the succession work. I'm also less sure how much of my certainty is judgment and how much is loyalty, so I'll disclose that history and let the lead director and counsel weigh it.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, and he resigned under pressure before any findings. Making that resignation final now would be a verdict without a record, and I'd say that about any CEO. Second, we never did serious succession work, and I'm not confident the interim can hold the company for long. Locking in a permanent exit during an AI market sell-off carries a continuity cost the board hasn't mitigated. Third, customers, partners and engineers follow him personally, but I'm holding that as one input rather than the lead argument. If the ordinary-course reporting doesn't support it, I'll drop it, and I'll tell the lead director that my loyalty to him may be coloring how much weight I give it.

## What would change your mind?
Written advice from counsel that an open door itself damages a license, a government relationship or our export compliance would change it, and I'd have to follow that. So would findings that he knowingly compromised sensitive information or misled this board. I'd also change my view if the interim assessment shows the company is being held back by the uncertainty and a credible search can't proceed with the door open. And if the name-swap test shows I'm reacting to him rather than to the principle, I'd step back from leading on this vote.
