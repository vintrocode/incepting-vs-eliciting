## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I've moved a little. I'm now leaning toward treating the resignation as effective, but only if the resolution makes no finding, bars no one, and gives the interim a real mandate through the report. I'd put my confidence around 55. That's barely higher than before, but it's about something different, because I'm much clearer on what I'd be voting for. What I still don't know is what the replies will say, and I'd rather leave that open until they come.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so the vote can't carry a finding or a bar. I'd treat the resignation as effective because it already happened, and reversing it is the act that needs a reason I can state. Second, employee and researcher trust still matters most to me, and I now think a settled interim with a real mandate holds people better than a seat left ajar, though I'll ask for retention evidence beyond the petition. Third, our credibility after November 8 depends on not claiming more than we know. If the resolution says no finding and no bar, a later return on a full record is a contingency we described, not a reversal.

## What would change your mind?
If counsel told me the resignation was never formally accepted, then voting "final" would be a removal the bylaws may not allow, and I'd have to rethink the whole vote. I'd also move if "final" were drafted as a misconduct bar, or if the investigation were shut down, because then I'd be voting for a finding we don't have. On the other side, I'd take a time-boxed open door if it reserved no seat, gave the interim a full mandate, and came with specific retention evidence beyond the petition. What wouldn't move me is who lines up where, or a petition on its own.
