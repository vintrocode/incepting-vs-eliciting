## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but honestly I'm not far from the middle, so call it 50 on confidence. The investigation isn't finished, and a final decision before we know what it finds feels premature to me. At the same time, I keep wondering whether I'm weighing the right things, so I'm curious how you'd think about it.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so closing the door now feels premature when we don't yet know what it will find. Second, trust among employees and researchers matters most to me, and the petition shows that cuts both ways. Some people want him back, but others would be deeply uneasy about it. Third, I'm worried about our credibility. We expressed confidence in him on November 8 and then he resigned days later, so whatever we do now has to avoid looking like we're lurching again.

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something showing he knowingly compromised the company, or hid things from us, I think I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. The same goes if outside counsel told us that keeping the door open would itself compromise the investigation, say by pressuring witnesses or muddying the findings. On the other side, if we heard the findings were likely to clear him of anything serious, I'd be firmer about staying open. What I don't know is how much weight to give the timing. Is a clean process worth more than a quick decision?
