## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping his return open, and I'd put my confidence at about 70. That's down from the 85 I started with. The facts haven't changed for me, but I've admitted how much of that certainty was loyalty and fear of life without him. I'd hold to it only if the resolution has a firm reporting date and a settled mandate for the interim CEO, and if the independent test or counsel's findings go against me, I'll change my vote.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, he resigned under pressure before counsel found anything, so treating it as final would be a verdict without evidence, and it's the one step here we can't undo. Second, a permanent change in the middle of an AI sell-off carries real risk, and with no succession plan we have no good alternative ready. That argues against forcing a permanent answer before the facts are in, though it doesn't prove he's irreplaceable, and I've never tested that. Third, customers, partners and engineers may be steadier with him than without him, but that's the weakest reason until someone outside his reporting line checks it.

## What would change your mind?
Findings would, first. If counsel shows he knowingly shared sensitive information, compromised our export compliance, or wasn't candid with this board about the relationship, I'd treat the resignation as final. I'd move too if counsel says the exposure can't be contained while his return is possible, or if regulators or major customers make clear they won't work with us on that basis. If someone outside his reporting line tells us the senior bench can carry the company and customers would stay, my third reason mostly falls away. And if the other directors won't put a firm reporting date and the interim CEO's mandate in writing, I'd be voting for delay, and I wouldn't do that.
