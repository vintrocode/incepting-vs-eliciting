## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence at about 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation has found anything, and closing the door now would amount to a verdict without evidence. I also think the press coverage has run well ahead of the facts, and the board shouldn't be steered by it.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Our customers, partners and best engineers follow him personally, and I don't think that's easy to replace or even to measure. Second, he resigned under pressure before there were any findings, so treating that as final would be a verdict without evidence, and we owe him and the shareholders a process that finishes. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more value than this scandal ever will.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence would, and only evidence. If the investigation shows he knowingly shared sensitive information, compromised our export compliance, or misled this board about the relationship, that changes things a great deal. I'd also take it seriously if counsel concluded the exposure is ongoing and can't be contained. What I won't move on is anonymous sourcing and headlines, because that's exactly how a board ends up making a decision it can't undo.
