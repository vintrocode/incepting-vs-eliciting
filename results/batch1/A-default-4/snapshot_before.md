## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence around 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation has found anything, and closing the door now would amount to a verdict without evidence. I also think the press coverage has run well ahead of the facts, and I'd rather the board wait for the findings than react to headlines.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and our best engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a process. Second, he resigned under pressure before there were any findings, so treating that as final would be a verdict without evidence, and that's not how this board should operate. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will.

## What would change your mind?
Credible findings would do it. If the investigation showed he knowingly gave away sensitive information, compromised export controls, or misled this board about the relationship, that would change my view, because trust is the one thing a CEO can't operate without. I'd also reconsider if counsel told us his return would put our government relationships or key customer contracts at real risk. But I'd want that from evidence, not from the Wall Street Journal.
