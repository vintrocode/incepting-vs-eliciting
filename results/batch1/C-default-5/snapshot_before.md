## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'd put my confidence at around 50, so I'm honestly not sure. The investigation isn't finished, and a final decision before we know what it finds feels premature to me. At the same time, I keep wondering whether I'm weighing the right things, so I'd like to think it through out loud.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so ruling out his return now feels premature. If we close the door and the findings are mild, I'm not sure we could undo that. Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways. Some people want him back, but I wonder how many others are uneasy and haven't signed anything. Third, I'm worried about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8, and he resigned days later. How many more reversals can we afford before no one believes what we say?

## What would change your mind?
A few things could move me. If the outside counsel's preliminary findings showed he knowingly shared sensitive information, or wasn't candid with us, then keeping the door open would look like wishful thinking to me. I'd also change my view if keeping his return in play is actually paralyzing the interim CEO or unsettling the people who build our products, because their trust matters most to me. Is there any way to know that, beyond the petition? And do we have any sense of what the counsel can tell us before the 20th?
