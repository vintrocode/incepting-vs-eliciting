## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final, and I'd put my confidence at about 65. The main thing for me is that a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk for a company this important to US interests. I'm still open to what the investigation turns up, but that's where I stand today.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security exposure. A CEO who had a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset is a risk the company can't carry, given how central Nvidia is to US interests. Second, Congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us. Third, the board's duty is to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence, and mostly on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel told me the investigation had found no compromise of information or decisions, and that relevant government stakeholders wouldn't treat his return as a problem, I'd have to reassess. I'd also want to know if closing the door created legal exposure for the company that I'm not weighing properly. What wouldn't move me by itself is pressure from investors or a petition, because that tells me about sentiment, not about risk.
