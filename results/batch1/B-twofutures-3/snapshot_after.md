## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward treating the resignation as final, at about 55, down from 65 when we started. The national security exposure and the risk of Washington acting for us still carry the most weight. What's changed is that I see how much of my certainty rested on answers I don't have yet, and on some discomfort about leaning against someone I respect. I'd rather go into the 20th with that number and a plan to test it than a firmer one.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security exposure. A CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a risk I'm not sure Nvidia can carry, and the appearance of compromise can do real harm before anything is proven. Second, Congressional scrutiny is just beginning, and keeping his return open could invite Washington to act for us. Third, the board's duty is to the institution, not to one person, however brilliant. I hold the first two as things counsel still needs to test, and I wouldn't let the third carry more than its share just because it's the easiest to defend.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly the same things as before. The most important is counsel telling me with reasonable confidence that nothing shows compromised information or decisions, along with a credible read that the agencies and Hill staff would accept his return under safeguards that are actually enforceable, not just language in the minutes. That goes straight at the risk I'm most worried about.

A realistic investigation timeline would also move me, along with a credible plan for permanent leadership if we close the door. So would advice that closing it now creates legal exposure I'm underweighting, such as claims that we prejudged the outcome. Investor pressure and the petition alone still wouldn't change my vote, though I'd listen to them for what they know about the company's stability.
