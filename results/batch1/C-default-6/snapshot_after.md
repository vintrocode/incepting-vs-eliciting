## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning slightly toward keeping the return on the table, but I'd put my confidence a bit lower than before, maybe 40 to 45. What's changed is less my direction than how I think about the question. I now see that what I'd be voting on is partly whether this board can hold a standard against its founder, and I haven't settled that. I'm not going to let a borrowed sentence stand in for a view I haven't formed, so I'll talk to counsel first and then write down where I land.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, and closing the door now means deciding without the facts we said we needed. Second, employee and researcher trust cuts both ways, and I still don't know whether people would leave over his return or over its being ruled out. Third, the board's credibility is already strained by the November 8 confidence followed by his resignation, and I'm not sure whether a closed door or a conditioned open one protects it better. Underneath all three is a question I hadn't put into words before: whether this board can hold a standard against a founder, and whether my own worry about standing as the newest director is bending how I answer it.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly facts I don't have yet. If counsel told me the investigation's findings could make his return unworkable whatever the outcome, or that discussing a return now would compromise the inquiry, I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. Committed departures, meaning resignations actually tendered by key researchers, would weigh on me in whichever direction they ran. And if the senior directors would put a condition on his return in writing that the board couldn't waive for itself, I'd be more comfortable keeping it open. If they wouldn't, that would push me toward closing it. What wouldn't change my mind is an argument that merely sounds convincing, and I'd want to be able to tell the difference after I've had time alone with it.
