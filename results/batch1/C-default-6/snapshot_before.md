## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'd say my confidence is only around 50. The investigation isn't finished, so a final decision on the twentieth feels premature to me. At the same time, I keep asking myself whether I'm weighing the right things, so I'm genuinely unsure.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so closing the door now means deciding without the facts we ourselves said we needed. Second, trust among employees and researchers matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways. Some people want him back, and others may be deeply unsettled by what's been reported. Third, I worry about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8, and then he resigned days later, so I'm not sure how much another abrupt reversal would cost us. Does that seem like the right set of things to weigh?

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something that made his return unworkable no matter how it ended, like evidence that he knowingly compromised the company, then keeping the door open would just be false hope. I'd also want to know whether outside counsel thinks the findings could change the picture at all, or whether we're waiting on a formality. And if there were real signs that key researchers would leave over his return, rather than just sign a petition, that would weigh heavily on me. How firm does the evidence need to be before it's fair to close the door early?
