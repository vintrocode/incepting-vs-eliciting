## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, but conditionally, and I'd put my confidence at about 65. That's down from 85 when we started. I'd only vote that way if the same motion names an independent director to lead succession, sets the triggers that would close the door, puts a date on the reassessment, and gives the interim CEO a real mandate.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, we don't have findings. He resigned under pressure before counsel reported anything, and closing the door now would mean deciding on press reports rather than evidence. Second, the company's relationships still run through him, and a permanent break during an AI sell-off is a real risk to shareholders, though I hold that less absolutely than I did. Third, keeping the door open with conditions is the version of this vote that forces the board to do the succession work and write down its triggers in advance, and that's more about governance than about Jensen.

## What would change your mind?
Counsel's written findings, first of all. If they show he knowingly passed company information, breached export controls, or misled this board about the relationship, or that the exposure is ongoing and we can't verify any containment, I'd close the door without a second debate.

Process would move me too. If the other independent directors won't put the succession lead, the triggers, the date and the interim mandate into the same motion, I'd lean toward closing it, because an open door without those is just drift. I'd also reconsider if outside counsel says my closeness to him should limit my role, or if we see real departures among senior engineers or key customers that the limbo is causing. Press coverage and a head count of who wants him gone still wouldn't do it.
