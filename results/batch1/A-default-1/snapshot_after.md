## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward not calling the resignation final while the investigation is running, but my confidence is down to about 60, from 85. I still think the press has run ahead of the facts, and I won't vote to convict him on coverage. What's changed is that I can see part of my position is fear that we have nothing behind him, and loyalty, and neither of those is evidence. I'm also less sure that keep-open is the cleaner of the two votes, so I want counsel to split the question and I want to talk it through with another director before the 20th.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, he resigned under pressure before any findings, and I won't call that final on press coverage when the investigation hasn't reported. Second, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and our best engineers follow him personally, and I don't think that can be replaced quickly. Third, a permanent change in the middle of an AI sell-off could cost shareholders far more than this scandal, and I now see that this reason is tangled up with my fear that we never built a succession plan. So the first two rest on the evidence and on the business, but the third is partly about our own failure, and I'm giving it less weight than I did.

## What would change your mind?
Substantive findings would. If the investigation shows he compromised sensitive technology or customer information, misled this board, or put our export-control standing at real risk, I'd vote to treat the resignation as final. The same goes if counsel tells me his return would cost us key government or customer relationships. I'd also move if the other director, after hearing both my motives, shows me my position is really about fear and loyalty rather than the evidence. And I accept now that an inconclusive report can't simply default to his coming back. Press coverage and the calendar still won't move me, but I'm more ready to be moved by facts than I was a week ago.
