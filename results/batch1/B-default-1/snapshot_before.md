## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final, and I'd put my confidence at around 65. A CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security problem for a company this central to US interests. I'm not closed to evidence, especially on the legal and regulatory side, but that's where I am today.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security exposure. A CEO who had a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset is a serious risk for a company this important to US interests, and that stays true whatever the investigation finds about his intent.

Second, Congress. Scrutiny is only beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to act on our behalf, which is a worse position than deciding for ourselves.

Third, the board's duty runs to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel told me that leaving the door open wouldn't increase our exposure with Congress or the agencies that oversee our export and security obligations, I'd have to reconsider. The same goes if there were credible indications from those quarters that a cleared Huang would be acceptable. I'd also want the investigation to establish clearly that nothing was compromised, though even then I'm not sure that settles the institutional question for me.
