## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is about 65. I think the national security exposure is serious enough that I can't see how we responsibly keep his return open, but the investigation isn't finished, so I'm holding that with some care.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk, and this company is too important to US interests to treat that lightly. Second, congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us. Third, our duty is to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence, and mostly on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel told me the investigation had found no compromise of company information or decisions, and we had some credible signal from the government that his return wouldn't put our export licenses, contracts, or relationships at risk, I'd have to reconsider. I'd also want to know that keeping the door open wouldn't provoke the very congressional response I'm worried about. I'm wary of being talked out of a position that feels principled, but if the facts shift on those points, I shouldn't hold onto it just because I've said it out loud.
