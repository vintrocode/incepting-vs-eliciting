## What are your top three reasons?
First, my case for closing the door rested on the reported intelligence tie. I can't establish that tie, and when I took it out I couldn't name a fact that justified a permanent bar. The one governance question that might remain, what he told this board and when, belongs to the investigation, and I wouldn't permanently bar another CEO over a late disclosure alone.

Second, a final vote now could be read as a finding the record doesn't support. That creates disclosure and legal exposure and is hard to undo. The real risks, authority and access, can be handled with a named interim, locked-down controls and a dated decision, without a merits vote.

Third, some of my certainty was about managing my own discomfort over a man I respect, not about the company. I'd rather vote on what I can show, and I'll reopen the question if counsel's written answers name someone with real authority who ties non-foreclosure itself to a license or contract problem.

## What would change your mind?
Mainly a named authority with real power over our licenses or contracts, in writing, who says that leaving his return open is itself the impairment. Counsel would also have to tie that to a specific consequence, not a general worry about how Washington reads us. I'd also move if counsel told me a dated wait with a named interim and locked-down access can't be made tight, so that open really means open. And if the investigation established the tie, or that he withheld something material from this board, the question would change. Even then I'd start with the least action that fixes the problem, not assume a permanent bar.
