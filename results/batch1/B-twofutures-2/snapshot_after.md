## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping the door open, with a written trigger that bites. My confidence is about 55 out of 100. That depends on counsel confirming nothing forces a vote on the twentieth, on what the findings show about the second reported relationship, and on whether this board will actually hold to the conditions. If those don't come through, I'm back to treating the resignation as final.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, closing the door now rests on a conclusion the investigation hasn't reached. The vote decides whether we're allowed to consider his return, and shutting that off early is hard to undo, especially with no succession plan and an interim CEO who can't lead with real authority.

Second, my duty is to the institution, and that means acting on evidence and holding a standard we set ourselves. An open door with a written trigger and early briefings to Washington lets us say we waited for findings and enforced a line, instead of ruling in advance.

Third, I had to admit that part of my pull toward "final" was relief and a wish to spare us a hard process, not judgment. The national security concern hasn't gone away, though. It sits at the center of the trigger, and it's why I'm only at 55.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence that company information or decisions were compromised, or that Jensen isn't cooperating fully, including on the second reported relationship, would send me straight back to treating the resignation as final. So would counsel telling me that agencies or Congress would see an open door as continuing exposure even with safeguards. If the board won't agree to a trigger specific enough that we couldn't quietly redefine it, I'd conclude the open door is just the investor-pressure outcome with better paperwork, and I'd go back to around 60 for final. Pressure from investors or the petition still wouldn't move me either way, only the findings, counsel's read on Washington, and whether this board will hold its own line.
