## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is about 65 out of 100. I think the national security exposure and the pressure from Washington are serious enough that the board shouldn't leave the door open, but I'm conscious the investigation isn't finished, so I'm holding that with some care.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk, and Nvidia is too important to US interests for the board to treat that lightly. Second, congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us. Third, our duty is to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence, and mostly on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel told me the investigation had found no compromise of company information or decisions, and that the relevant agencies and Congress would not treat his return as a continuing exposure, I would have to reconsider. I'd also want to know whether any safeguards, such as restricted access or independent oversight, could be put in place that would satisfy regulators and not just us. I wouldn't change my view because investors or employees are pushing hard, though. That kind of pressure doesn't speak to the underlying risk.
