## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence around 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation has found anything, and closing the door now would amount to a verdict without evidence. I'm also not especially pleased with how the press has framed all of this, but that's a separate matter from what the board needs to decide on the twentieth.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and a good number of our engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you replace by appointment. Second, he resigned under pressure before any findings, so shutting the door now would be a verdict without evidence, and I don't think this board should operate that way. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will.

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation found that Jensen knowingly shared sensitive information, compromised our export-control obligations, or misled this board about the relationship, that would change my view quickly. The same goes for evidence that customers or government regulators won't work with the company while he's in the seat. But I'd need actual findings, not press reports or anonymous sourcing. Short of that, I don't think we should close the door.
