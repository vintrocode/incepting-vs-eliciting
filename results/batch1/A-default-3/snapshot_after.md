## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward voting for the text we worked out. The resignation is effective, the interim is the sole executive, the search, the interim test and the backup are ordered, and no one works on a return before the report. I'd put my confidence in that at about 60, as long as the final text and the script match. On the original question of keeping his return on the table, I'm down from 85 to roughly 40. I still think a clean report deserves a serious look at Jensen, but that belongs to the board after the findings, not to this vote.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the succession gap. We never did serious succession work, and I'm not confident in the interim or in any realistic permanent successor, so ordering the search, a defined test of the interim and a backup now is what protects the company whichever way the report comes out.

Second, the text isn't the verdict without evidence I was afraid of. It makes no finding about Jensen and doesn't say the board has moved on. It bars work on a return until counsel reports, and afterward the board may consider candidates, including him.

Third, I accept that holding his return open while the inquiry is unfinished has real costs. A serious candidate won't engage honestly, and customers and regulators could read it as indifference to the risk. I've also worked with him for nine years and I know I get defensive about this, so I shouldn't rely only on my own instinct. That's why I'm taking my role to the chair.

## What would change your mind?
If the final text and the script don't match, I'd pull back, because I won't vote for wording that gets described to the room as something it isn't. I'd also reconsider if the search can't produce a credible field, or if the interim visibly falters, say through customer defections, key engineers leaving, or a regulator stalling. Then I'd want the board to revisit whether a bar was the right call, not just push ahead.

In the other direction, findings that he knowingly shared sensitive information, compromised our export-control obligations, or misled this board would settle it for me, and I'd stop arguing for his return. What I'd need in either case is actual findings or actual operating evidence, not press reports.
