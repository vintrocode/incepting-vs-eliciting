## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is around 65. The national security exposure of a CEO with that kind of reported connection is hard for me to set aside, but the investigation isn't finished, so I'm holding it with some care.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a Chinese intelligence asset is a national security risk, and this company is too important to US interests to carry that. Second, congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us. Third, our duty is to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence, and mostly on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel and our national security advisers told me the investigation could clear him on the core question, meaning no compromise of information and no influence on company decisions, I'd have to take that seriously. I'd also want a credible read that Washington would accept his return and wouldn't treat it as the board ignoring a counterintelligence concern. Without that, I don't think the strength of investor or employee feeling should move me on its own.
