## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, with a deadline and written criteria, and my confidence is about 50. The number hasn't moved, but what sits behind it has. I know what would change my mind, and I know what I'll do before the 20th: send the email to the chair, state my line, and ask counsel about the conflict question and what the findings are likely to look like.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, and I don't want to close the door before we have the facts we commissioned it to give us. Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways, so I can't treat either reaction as the last word. Third, the board's credibility after November 8 and his resignation days later: any decision should rest on a standard we set before seeing the findings, not on press reports or whoever is pushing hardest.

The third carries the most weight for me now, partly because it's the one I can act on before the 20th. It also points to what I think the vote is really about, which is whether this board can hold a founder to a standard at all.

## What would change your mind?
A finding that he compromised our technology or relationships, or that he wasn't fully candid with us, would move me to treating the resignation as final, whatever the investors or the petition want. So would counsel telling me the findings are months away and the interim CEO can't lead while his return is open, because then waiting has its own cost. I'd also move if the board refused both a deadline and any standard for ambiguous findings, since then an open door would just be deference to him. What shouldn't move me is the senior directors' history with him or how settled the room feels, and I'd want to catch myself if either started doing the work that evidence should do.
