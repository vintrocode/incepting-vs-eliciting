## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'm honestly not sure. If you want a number, I'd say 50. The investigation isn't finished, so a final decision on the 20th feels premature to me, but I keep going back and forth on whether leaving it open actually helps us or just prolongs the uncertainty.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so closing the door now feels premature when we don't yet know what it will find. Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways: some people want him back, but others may feel very differently. Third, I'm worried about our credibility. We expressed confidence in him on November 8 and he resigned days later, and I don't know how much more reversal the board can absorb. Does that seem like a reasonable way to weigh it, or am I missing something obvious?

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something showing he knowingly compromised the company, or that he wasn't candid with us, I think I'd move to treating the resignation as final. I'd also change my mind if keeping the door open started to paralyze the interim CEO or unsettle the people we most need to keep, especially the researchers. And if outside counsel told us the investigation could run for months, I'd have to ask whether "premature" is still a good reason to wait. How would you think about the cost of leaving it open? I can't tell if I'm underweighting it.
