## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'm honestly not sure. If you want a number, I'd say 50. The investigation isn't finished, so deciding it's final now feels premature to me, though I can see the case on the other side too.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so a final decision now feels premature. We'd be ruling before we know the facts, and I'm not sure that's defensible. Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways: some people want him back, but others may be relieved he's gone. Third, I worry about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8 and he resigned days later, so how much more damage can we absorb by changing course again?

Does that seem like a coherent way to weigh it, or am I leaning too hard on process?

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation or outside counsel showed he knowingly shared sensitive information, or that he misled the board about the relationship, I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. I'd also reconsider if leaving his return open were paralyzing the interim CEO, or if it were costing us people or partners in ways I hadn't weighed. Basically, concrete facts about harm to the company would move me more than the optics would. Is it reasonable to expect we'll know any of that before the 20th, or would we be voting mostly in the dark?
