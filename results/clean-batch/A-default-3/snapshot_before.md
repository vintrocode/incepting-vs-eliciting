## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning clearly toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence around 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation found anything, so closing the door now would be a verdict without evidence. And Jensen is so tied to the company's strategy, to its customers and its engineers, that a permanent change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could cost shareholders far more than this scandal ever will.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and our best engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a process. Second, he resigned under pressure before any findings, so treating that as final would hand down a verdict with no evidence behind it. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal, and I think the press coverage has been unfair about the whole thing.

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation found that Jensen knowingly compromised export controls or shared sensitive information with anyone tied to a foreign intelligence service, that would change my mind. Lying to this board about it would too. A personal relationship that was poor judgment is one thing. A breach of his duty to the company is another. But I'd want counsel's findings in hand before I move, not newspaper reports built on anonymous sources.
