## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final for now, with any return reopened as a fresh decision after the findings. My confidence in that is about 60. Put the other way, I'm at roughly 35 on keeping his return on the table, down from 50.

The move came from two reasons I can defend. Nobody has named who would enforce conditions against him, and a board shouldn't bargain with someone it's still investigating. I'd still hold it loosely until I've heard counsel's view on the investigation and seen the strongest written version of a conditional path. I also want one of the senior directors to argue the other side to me first.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, nobody has named who would enforce conditions against him, and I can't vote for a path back that depends on a promise no one owns. Second, a board shouldn't be negotiating terms with someone it's still investigating. The unfinished investigation, which I first used to argue for keeping the door open, is the very reason we can't set those terms yet. Third, credibility. After the November 8 statement and his resignation, a vague open door or conditions we fail to enforce would read as a pattern to the researchers and employees I care most about. "Final for now," with any return decided fresh after the findings, is something we can explain in the same words in public.

## What would change your mind?
I'd move back toward keeping it open if someone put a real conditional path on paper before the 20th. That means conditions tied to the findings, a named enforcer who isn't tied to him by years of loyalty, a defined consequence if he doesn't meet them, and counsel confirming it won't make witnesses less candid. I'd also move if I learned that senior researchers would actually leave over a closed door, not just sign a petition, or if counsel said the leadership gap under the interim CEO is a bigger risk than I've assumed. And if a senior director makes the strongest case for the conditional path and my two grounds don't hold up against it, I'd want to say so honestly rather than defend a position out of pride.
