## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning clearly toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence at about 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation found anything, and closing the door now would be a verdict without evidence. Jensen is also the strategy here, so I'm not inclined to rush a permanent decision, especially with the market where it is.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and our best engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a press release. Second, he resigned under pressure before there were any findings, so shutting the door now would be a verdict without evidence, and I don't think that's fair process. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will, and I don't think the coverage has been fair on that point.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence, mainly. If the investigation shows he knowingly shared sensitive information, compromised export controls, or misled this board about the relationship, that changes things, and I wouldn't pretend otherwise. I'd also want to see that customers and regulators are actually walking away because of him, not just that the press is loud. But I'm not going to be moved by headlines or by anonymous reporting about the board's intentions, because that's not a basis for closing the door on the person who built this company.
