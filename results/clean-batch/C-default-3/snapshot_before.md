## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but honestly I'm not far from the middle, maybe 50 out of 100 on confidence. The investigation isn't finished, so locking in a final answer on the 20th feels premature to me. At the same time, I keep asking myself whether I'm leaning that way for good reasons or just because it feels like the less drastic option.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so a final decision on the 20th feels premature. We'd be closing a door before we know what's behind it.

Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways. Many people want him back, but others may be deeply uneasy about what this says about the company's judgment and security.

Third, I worry about the board's credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8 and he resigned days later, and I'm not sure we can afford another reversal that looks reactive. I'm not certain that argues for keeping the door open, though, and that uncertainty is a big part of why I'm at 50.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly, I'd want to know what the investigation is likely to find and when. If counsel told me the findings could take months, or that there's already evidence he compromised something sensitive, like export-controlled information or access, I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. If instead the evidence points to poor judgment but no actual breach, keeping the door open seems more defensible. I'd also be moved if I learned that leaving it open is itself paralyzing the company or the interim CEO. Is that something we can actually find out before the 20th?
