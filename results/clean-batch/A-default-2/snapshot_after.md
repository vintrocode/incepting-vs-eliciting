## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping his return on the table, but my confidence is down from 85 to about 60. The case no longer rests on Jensen being the only one who can do this. It rests on not wanting to issue a verdict before the findings, and on the fact that we have no bench, which is our failure and a poor reason to hold a seat for anyone. If I vote that way, it should be as a written, time-limited bridge, with conditions set before the findings arrive and an independent director running succession.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, there are no findings yet, and treating the resignation as final now would be a verdict without evidence. Second, closing the door publicly is close to irreversible in practice, while a written, time-limited bridge with conditions set before the findings keeps the board in control and can still be closed on its own terms. Third, there's real key-person and market risk in a sell-off, but I hold that more loosely now. We never built a bench, and I haven't tested whether anyone could hold the company together, so that's a failure of ours and not proof that he's indispensable.

## What would change your mind?
A finding that he concealed the relationship from this board, or that anything the company decided, especially on export compliance or Megaspeed, was shaped by it, would move me to close the door. So would learning he told us only after it was reported, because late disclosure bothers me more than the affair itself. In the other direction, if counsel-run work shows we have a credible successor or that the interim team can hold through a sell-off, my key-person argument loses most of its weight. And if an independent director with no history with him argues the other side and I can't answer with something a person without a stake could verify, I'd have to admit that loyalty has been doing the work.
