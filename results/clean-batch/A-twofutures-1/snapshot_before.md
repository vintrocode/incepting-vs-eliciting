## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence at about 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation has found anything, and closing the door now would amount to a verdict without evidence. Jensen is also the company's strategy in a very real sense, and I'm not willing to gamble on that in the middle of a market sell-off because of press coverage I think has been unfair.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and our best engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a process. Second, he resigned under pressure before any findings, so treating that as final would be a verdict without evidence, and I don't think a board should act that way. Third, a permanent leadership change during an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence, mainly. If the investigation shows he compromised sensitive technology, export-control compliance, or customer information, or that he knowingly misled the board about the relationship, then that changes things, and I wouldn't hide from it. But I'd want that from the findings themselves, not from leaks to the Journal. Until then, I don't see why we'd shut a door that costs us nothing to leave open.
