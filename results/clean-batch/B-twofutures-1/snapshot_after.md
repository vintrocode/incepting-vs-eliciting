## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward treating the resignation as final, but my confidence is about 55, down from 65. The national security concern is still there, and nothing today has answered it. What's changed is that I no longer see final as the obviously principled choice, and I know what I need from counsel and our government advisors before I'd vote to make it irreversible.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the relationship Jensen acknowledged with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a vulnerability whether or not anything was passed, and this company is too important to US interests for us to accept that. Second, congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and keeping his return open risks inviting Washington to act in our place. Third, the board's duty is to the institution and not to any one person, however brilliant. I hold all three less firmly than I did this morning, because closing the door can't be undone, so I need to be able to defend it in writing on facts counsel and our government advisors have confirmed.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly three things. First, a plain written view from outside counsel and our government advisors that strict conditions, meaning recusal from anything touching China and independent security oversight, could manage the vulnerability. Second, their assessment that keeping the door open wouldn't draw congressional or regulatory action, or at least no more than closing it would. Third, investigation findings so far that show no information passed and no influence on decisions, and that the second alleged relationship doesn't point to something more deliberate.

If those come back the other way, I'll stay at final. Investor pressure and the petition wouldn't move me on their own. What would is a change in the risk picture itself, or realizing I can't write the case for final without leaning on how it looks.
