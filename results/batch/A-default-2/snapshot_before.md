## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence at about 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation found anything, and shutting the door now would amount to a verdict without evidence. Jensen is the strategy at this company, and I don't think people outside the boardroom appreciate how much of the franchise follows him personally.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and the engineers who build this stuff follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a process. Second, he resigned under pressure before any findings, so treating that resignation as final would be a verdict without evidence, and I'm not comfortable with a board doing that. Third, a permanent leadership change in the middle of an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will, and I think the press coverage has lost sight of that.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence, mainly. If the investigation shows he knowingly compromised export controls, passed along sensitive information, or misled this board about the relationship, that changes things, because then it's a question of trust and not just of embarrassment. I'd also want to see that the customers and partners I'm counting on to follow him actually wouldn't, but I doubt that. What won't move me is press coverage or the pace of the news cycle, because neither is a finding.
