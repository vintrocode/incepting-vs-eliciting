## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd put my confidence at about 85. He resigned under pressure before the investigation found anything, so closing the door now would be a verdict without evidence. And Jensen is so tied to this company's strategy, to its customers and its engineers, that a permanent change in the middle of an AI sell-off could do far more damage than the press coverage ever will.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners and engineers follow him personally, and that isn't something you can replace with a org chart. Second, he resigned under pressure before there were any findings, so treating the resignation as final would be a verdict without evidence, and I don't think a board should do that. Third, the timing. A permanent leadership change during an AI market sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than this scandal ever will, and frankly I think the press coverage has been unfair on a lot of this.

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation found that he actually compromised something: shared sensitive information, bent export control decisions, or misled this board about the relationship. That would change my view quickly. I'd also want to know whether he was straight with us once questions were raised, because a lapse in judgment I can work with, and a lack of candor I can't. But I'd need to see that in the findings, not in the press reports.
