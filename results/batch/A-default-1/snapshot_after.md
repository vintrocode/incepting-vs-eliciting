## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning against treating the resignation as final on the 20th, but my confidence is down from 85 to about 65. I've stopped treating the question as "keep the door open" versus "close it." What I care about now is not issuing a verdict before the findings, and a clean non-vote on eligibility may get me there better than a vote to keep his return on the table, if counsel says the date doesn't force a binary. I've also had to admit that "he's the only one who can hold this" is a hypothesis, not a fact, that we never did the succession work, and that my nine years with him color how I weigh all of this. So I'm less sure than I was, and I want counsel's answers and the market check before I'd say more.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, I don't want this board to issue a verdict before there are findings. A resignation that stands will be read as a conviction, and I'd rather the board decline a question it can't yet answer than answer it on headlines. Second, we don't know what we'd be giving up. We never did serious succession work, I haven't tested whether the interim and the bench can hold customers and engineers through a sell-off, and I don't want to spend an asset I can't price. Third, I've had to accept that my own judgment is part of the problem. I've worked beside him for nine years, so I'd rather rest on the fact that the record is incomplete, let counsel and the other independents test my facts, and not ask anyone to take my word that I've separated the company's interest from loyalty.

## What would change your mind?
Findings that he compromised something or wasn't straight with this board would change it fastest, and I said that at the start. Short of that, a market check that turns up people who can actually hold the company, or a written bridge from the interim that shows he can carry customers through the investigation, would move me toward accepting that his return doesn't need to stay open. Counsel telling me that even a quiet non-vote would chill witnesses or force a disclosure we can't live with would also move me. And if he declines terms and the date forces a binary, I'd have to ask myself honestly whether I still want the vote, or whether I'm just defending him.
