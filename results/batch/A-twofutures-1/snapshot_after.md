## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping his return on the table, but with conditions attached, and I'd put my confidence at about 65. It's down from 85 because I can't fully separate my judgment from my loyalty, and I haven't heard what counsel has told the other directors. The principle that we shouldn't issue a verdict before the findings still holds for me. I just won't vote for a bare open door anymore.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, we shouldn't issue a verdict before the findings. He resigned under pressure, and treating that as final would mean deciding without evidence. Second, Jensen is still central to the strategy. Customers, partners and engineers follow him personally, and a permanent change during this sell-off carries real risk, especially with no succession work behind it. Third, an open door with a firm end date, a written mandate for the interim CEO, and Jensen kept clear of the investigation and any operating role costs us little, and it keeps the decision tied to the facts. I'd add that the succession gap is not one of my reasons for him. It's a failure of this board, and I don't want it standing in for an argument.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly the findings. If they show he knew more about Alice Huang and Megaspeed than he told us, misled the board, or exposed anything sensitive, such as export-control compliance, technology or customer information, I'd vote to close the door. The same goes if his on-the-record account doesn't hold up against what counsel finds. I'd also take seriously a view from counsel, or from one of the directors who raised the conflict, that my closeness to him is compromising my judgment. And if the board won't accept the end date, the interim CEO's mandate and Jensen staying clear of the investigation, I wouldn't vote for a bare open door. I'd also want to hear from Washington and our largest customers, because so far I've mostly heard from the people who want him back.
