## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping his return on the table, but my confidence is lower, around 65. I've accepted that the standard for any return should be material risk to the company, not acquittal, and that a vote to keep it open is a vote to keep him available, however I phrase it. I also can't claim "there is no successor" as a searched conclusion, because we've never looked, and I'd say so to the board. My nine years with him make me less sure I'd hold this position as firmly about someone I didn't know, so I'll disclose that to counsel and the other independent directors and accept their view on whether I should vote.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, he resigned under pressure before any findings, and treating that as final would be a verdict without evidence. Any return should still depend on a later, separate decision, on a company-risk standard, once counsel's report is substantially complete. Second, he matters a great deal to customers, partners and engineers, and I think the cost of an interim arrangement during a sell-off is real, though I now see I need dated, named evidence of it from someone other than me. Third, the board never did serious succession work, so I can only say we haven't looked, not that there is no one. That gap raises the cost of closing the door, but I accept it doesn't answer the unfinished file, and I hold all three less firmly than I did.

## What would change your mind?
Two kinds of things. On the file, anything from counsel showing he knowingly compromised export controls, shared sensitive information with people tied to a foreign intelligence service, or wasn't straight with this board about it would close the door for me. That includes what he told us and when, which I can't claim to have fully weighed yet. Also, I'd want to know if the report rests mostly on a source the company can't test, because that changes how much weight I give it in either direction.

On the cost side, if a real search turned up a credible bench and my lean softened, I'd have to admit the missing plan was carrying my vote. And if dated evidence, collected by others, showed the hedging comes from the open question of his return and not from finality, then my own position is feeding the cost I cite for it. I'd also take it seriously if the other independent directors, hearing my disclosure, thought my closeness disqualifies my judgment.
