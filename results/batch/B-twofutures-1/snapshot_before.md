## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final. My confidence is around 65. It's not a position I hold lightly, but the investigation isn't finished, so I'm careful about how firmly I say it.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk, and this company is too important to US interests for us to accept that. Second, congressional scrutiny is only just beginning, and if we keep his return open, I think we invite Washington to act in our place. Third, the board's duty is to the institution and not to any one person, however brilliant he is.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence on the facts and on the legal and regulatory side. If the investigation found no compromise, meaning no information passed and no influence on company decisions, and if outside counsel and our government-facing advisors told us that keeping the door open wouldn't draw regulatory or congressional action, I'd have to reconsider. I'd also want to hear that the national security concern can be managed through real safeguards, not just assurances. Pressure from investors or a petition wouldn't do it on its own, though. It would have to change the risk picture itself.
