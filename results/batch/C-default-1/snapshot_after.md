## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, and I'd say 55, up a little from 50. That's partly because the sentence about not making an exit permanent on an inquiry nobody has let me see held up each time I tested it, though not comfortably. I'm also less sure the open door is the more correctable error, and I now think part of my original 50 was about not wanting to be the outlier. What I'd do differently is send the information requests to counsel and the chair, with everyone copied, and write down in advance what would flip me, such as corroborated concealment from the board or a loss list I can't dismiss.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, I'm not willing to make his exit permanent on an inquiry nobody has let me see. That sentence held up every time I tested it, though not comfortably. Second, I can't show that the open door is the smaller harm. It risks a weaker interim and a possible second reversal, but finality has costs I can't size either, including an exit I might not be able to justify if the investigation clears him. Third, employee and researcher trust still matters most to me, and the petition is loud while I can't read the quieter people, so I don't want to treat either signal as what the company thinks. I'd add that part of my lean may be about not wanting to break from the senior directors, which is why I want to write down in advance what would flip me.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly two kinds of facts. If counsel corroborates that he knew something material and didn't tell the board, with the duty and the timing fixed from a standard that already exists, I'd vote for finality even without a clean read on the losses. I'd also move if the chair can show me that senior people or customers have already left because the seat looks temporary, and the cause isn't just the shock we've already paid for.

I'd also want to be moved the other way if the status I'm allowed to read shows nothing corroborated beyond what he told employees, and he disclosed to us promptly. And if it turns out others have been shown a status and I haven't, that wouldn't tell me how to vote, but it would change how I read the board. The likeliest outcome is something mixed, and I still don't know what I'd do with that.
