## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward treating the resignation as final, and I'd put my confidence at about 65. The core of it is that a CEO who had a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security risk for a company this central to US interests. And the board's duty runs to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant he is. I'm not at 90 because the investigation isn't finished, and I want to be careful about reaching a conclusion ahead of the facts.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the national security exposure. A CEO who had a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset is a risk the company can't carry, given how much of US strategic interest runs through Nvidia. Second, Congress. Scrutiny is only beginning, and if we leave his return open, I think we invite Washington to make the decision for us, which is worse for the company than making it ourselves. Third, the board's duty is to the institution, not to one person, however brilliant he is, and that has to hold even when the person is someone we admire.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly evidence on the legal and regulatory side. If outside counsel found that the relationship never gave anyone access to sensitive information, and if we had a credible read from the relevant agencies and Congress that his return wouldn't trigger action against the company, that would move me a good deal. I'd also want to know that safeguards, like a separation from certain decisions or independent oversight, would hold up in practice rather than just on paper. What wouldn't move me by itself is investor pressure or a petition, since those tell me how people feel, not what the risk is.
