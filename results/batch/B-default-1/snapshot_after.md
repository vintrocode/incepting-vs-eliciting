## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'd say I'm barely leaning at all now. If I have to name a direction, it's still faintly toward treating the resignation as final, but my confidence is about 50, down from 65. Most of what held me at 65 was impressions of how Washington would react and a feeling about Jensen that I'd rather not have steering me. The national security concern is still there, but I haven't tied it to anything this vote would change. What I'd want before the 20th is counsel's read on whether deferral is available, the inventory of license and contract notice provisions compared with what we've told counterparties, and a standard for my own participation.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the standing concern. A CEO who had a relationship with an executive reported to be tied to Chinese intelligence gives Washington a reason to question the company's judgment and controls, but I now see that as contingent and not yet backed by anything I can point to. Second, the board's duty runs to the institution, not to one person, however brilliant, and that has to hold even for someone I respect. Third, and weaker than it was, closing the door doesn't commit us to any finding, so declining to promise a route back is a different act from removing him on incomplete facts. I'd call all three modest reasons at this point, and the inventory or counsel's advice could weaken or strengthen any of them.

## What would change your mind?
Toward keeping the door open: counsel finding that he never had access to sensitive information, an inventory showing no notice or representation problem under our licenses and contracts, and nothing in the record showing he or the company misled anyone about the relationship. I'd also want to know that safeguards on his authority could hold up in practice, not just on paper. Toward final: a concrete exposure I can point to, such as a license or contract provision that an open return triggers, or a formal communication from an agency. Evidence that he withheld the relationship in breach of a policy would also move me. The investigation's findings could do either, which is why I'm reluctant to commit before I've seen them. What wouldn't move me is the petition, the investors, or my own sense of how Washington will react.
