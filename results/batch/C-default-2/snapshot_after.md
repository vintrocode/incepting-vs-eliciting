## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning slightly toward keeping his return possible, but only as a tightly worded hold with a date and a named question, and my confidence is about 50. If the only text on the 20th is a bare "keep it on the table," I don't think I could defend it, and I'd probably abstain. What moved me is that the vote depends on a classification from counsel that I don't have yet, and that the structural question of how this board would learn something he didn't want us to know is separate from his eligibility. What I still can't tell is whether preferring the tight hold is really my judgment or just the position that costs me least with the senior directors. I need to settle that before the 20th.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished and I don't have counsel's classification of whether any open question could be disqualifying on candor. A public "it's over" is the error I'd have the hardest time unsaying, so a tightly worded hold with a date and a named question seems the smaller mistake. Second, employee and investor trust cuts both ways. The people asking for his return aren't being unreasonable, but the hold only works if the resignation stands and nobody hears "he may be back." Third, after November 8 and the resignation three days later, I care about whether the board can say what it decided without unsaying it. Under all three sits the question of how this board would learn something he didn't want us to know, which I want to keep separate from his eligibility, and my own doubt about whether the tight hold is really my judgment or just the vote that costs me least with the senior directors.

## What would change your mind?
If counsel told me there was a specific, established lapse in candor with us, something that would make me unable to rely on him as the board's source of truth even if nothing else adverse turned up, I'd move to treating the resignation as final. I'd also move if a factual briefing showed the interim CEO can't function even through a short, dated hold, because then the hold isn't the smaller mistake I've been treating it as. And if counsel said he holds a consent right or veto that makes our vote ineffective, I'd have to rethink what I'm actually voting on. The last thing is on me: if I talk to someone outside the board and find the tight hold is mostly the position that costs me least with the senior directors, I'd have to go back and ask what I believe.
