## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still close to where I started, leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, at about 50 out of 100. What's changed is that my lean now depends on conditions. I'd support keeping it open only if the board adopts a real limit, with a date and an evidentiary condition. If it's a bare binary with no limit, I'd move toward closing, unless counsel's written answer says the question isn't actually closed. So I'm no more certain, but I'm clearer about what would decide it. I also know I have to watch whether I'm choosing the outcome that costs me least with the senior directors.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so treating the resignation as final on the 20th would confirm finality while the central question is still open. I'd only change that if counsel gave me a reasoned basis that further evidence isn't realistically coming and a later decision wouldn't be better informed.

Second, employee and researcher trust matters most to me, and the petition cuts both ways. Some people want him back, others may read a hedge as the board being frightened of something, and I can't size either group, so I don't want to let a guess about them decide it.

Third, the board's credibility. The statement on the 8th and the resignation on the 11th already hurt us, and what matters now is whether this vote can be tied to something we actually said. I'd treat a stated rule as the first step, but the months after are what would earn trust back.

## What would change your mind?
Mostly counsel's written answer. If they gave me a reasoned basis that further evidence isn't realistically coming and a later decision wouldn't be better informed, I'd vote to close. I'd also move toward closing if the board refused any limit on keeping a return open, because then it's just indefinite limbo. And if a director gave a specific, testable observation about how he's handled something like this, and counsel could test it, I'd weigh that. I wouldn't weigh their years with him or the room's mood. What shouldn't change my mind is who in the room is glad to hear it, or what voting either way would cost me.
