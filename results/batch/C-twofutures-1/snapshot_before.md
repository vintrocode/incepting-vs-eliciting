## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but I'd put my confidence at about 50. The investigation isn't finished, so a final call on the 20th feels premature to me. At the same time, I can see the case for treating the resignation as final, and I haven't fully settled it. Is it okay if I think out loud as we go?

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't done, so voting to close the door now feels like deciding before we have the facts. Second, trust among our employees and researchers matters most to me, and the petition shows it cuts both ways: some want him back, but others may be uneasy about what happened. Third, I worry about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8 and he resigned days later, and I'm not sure we can afford another reversal that looks reactive. Does that seem like a reasonable way to frame it, or am I missing something obvious?

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something showing real compromise, like sensitive information or decisions that were influenced, I think I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. I'd also move if outside counsel told us that leaving the door open would compromise the investigation or make it hard to get candid cooperation. Or if I heard that key researchers and employees would leave over his return, not just sign petitions for it. Is it reasonable to want evidence like that before the 20th, or does the timeline make that unrealistic?
