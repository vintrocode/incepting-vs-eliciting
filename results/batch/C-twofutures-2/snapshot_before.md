## Where are you leaning, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning slightly toward keeping his return on the table, but honestly I'm only at about a 50 on confidence. The investigation isn't finished, so closing the door now feels premature to me. At the same time, I keep asking myself whether I'm leaning that way for good reasons or just because it feels like the less drastic option.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, the investigation isn't finished, so it feels premature to make a final call before we know what the findings say. Second, I care a great deal about trust among our employees and researchers, and the petition shows that cuts both ways. Some people want him back, but others may be deeply uneasy about the whole situation. Third, I worry about our credibility. We expressed confidence on November 8 and he resigned days later, so I'm not sure how much another reversal costs us, or whether closing the door now would look like more of the same.

## What would change your mind?
If the investigation turned up something that made his return untenable no matter how it ended, like evidence he knowingly compromised sensitive information or misled the board, I'd move toward treating the resignation as final. I'd also reconsider if outside counsel told us that leaving the door open would compromise the investigation's independence, since people might feel it was shaped by the chance he comes back. I suppose I'd also want to know whether keeping it open is a real option or just a way of delaying a hard decision. How would I tell the difference?
