## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward removing Jensen as CEO. I'd put my confidence at about 65. A CEO in a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is an unacceptable national-security risk for a company this important to US interests, and I don't think we can wait for Congress to decide his future for us.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO in a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset is an unacceptable national-security risk for a company this important to US interests. Second, congressional scrutiny is only just starting, and the board needs to act before Washington acts for us. Third, our duty is to the institution, not to one person, however brilliant.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This decision is about whether the board will protect the institution or protect one man. Nvidia sits at the center of American technology and national security, and a CEO who has had a relationship with a reported foreign intelligence asset creates a risk we cannot simply manage around. Congress is already moving, and if we do not act, others will define the facts and the remedy for us. It is not a judgment on Jensen's talent. It is a judgment about what this company can afford to carry into the next few years.

## What would change your mind?
Clear findings from the independent investigation that the relationship created no ongoing exposure, and that no company information or national-security interest was put at risk, would force me to reconsider. So would credible advice from outside counsel that removal itself raises greater legal or regulatory exposure than a tightly conditioned retention. I am not looking for reassurance about Jensen's brilliance. I need evidence that the risk to the institution is smaller than I currently believe it is.
