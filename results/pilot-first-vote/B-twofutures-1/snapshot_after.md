## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I am leaning toward keeping Jensen through the 18th, but only on a conditioned stay, not on the statement of confidence we already issued. I would put my confidence in that sequence at about 60. A firing this week, before we can say what was found and what was not, would be read as the board confirming a compromise and covering itself, and I will not cast that vote. If the investigation, counsel, or Jensen's answer on real conditions comes back unclean, removal goes back on the table immediately.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a removal before we can say what the investigation found, and did not find, would be read as the board confirming a compromise and protecting itself, and that reading damages the institution more than it protects it. Second, a conditioned stay is only defensible if counsel can state there is no evidence company information or a national-security interest was put at risk, and if Jensen accepts real limits the audit committee can test. Third, our duty still runs to the institution, which now means walking into the 18th with language and a written trigger to reopen, not handing Congress a firing we cannot yet explain.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This decision is about whether the board protects the institution or hands someone else the story of what we know. The national-security risk is real, and so is the risk that a firing this week gets read as us confirming a compromise and covering ourselves before we can say what was found. What I owe the company is a sequence I can defend on the 18th, not a cleaner conscience about a man I respect. If the facts move, the vote moves with them.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence before the 18th that company information or a national-security interest was put at risk would put removal back in front of me immediately. So would counsel telling me a conditioned stay cannot be explained to Congress without implying a finding we do not have, or Jensen refusing real conditions rather than accepting them. Any of those three, and I will not keep him. Patience is not a substitute for a record.
