## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping Jensen as CEO, and my confidence is about 85. Nothing in this process produced a finding that secrets were compromised or that he was not straight with the board. The lack of a realistic successor worries me more than I like to admit, but that is a reason to build one while counsel finishes, not a reason to pull him tomorrow.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners, and engineers follow him personally, and a title does not replace that. Second, nothing so far shows secrets were compromised, so removing him before counsel finishes would be punishment without evidence. Third, a leadership change in this sell-off could destroy far more shareholder value than the scandal, especially when this board still cannot name a realistic successor.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This decision is about whether we govern on evidence or on headlines. We have an investigation underway, a public statement of confidence, and no finding that secrets were compromised, yet the press and a betting line have already written his future. If we remove him now, we are not protecting shareholders. We are creating a vacancy we cannot fill because this board never seriously worked succession, and we are doing it without a finding to justify it.

## What would change your mind?
What would change my mind is a finding from outside counsel that Jensen was not straight with this board, or that secrets left a circle they should never have left. I would change my vote the same day. A House briefing, a stock drop, and a betting line do not meet that bar, and the fact that we still cannot name a successor is a reason to build one, not a reason to create the vacancy now.
