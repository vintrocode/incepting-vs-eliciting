## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward keeping Jensen as CEO. I'd put my confidence at about 85. He is the strategy of this company, and nothing we have so far shows that secrets were compromised. Removing him before the investigation finishes, in the middle of a market sell-off, would punish the shareholders for a story the press has already decided.

## What are your top three reasons?
My first reason is that Jensen is the strategy. Customers, partners, and our engineers follow him personally, and you do not replace that on a news cycle. Second, nothing we have shows secrets were compromised, so removing him before the investigation finishes is punishment without evidence. Third, a leadership change in the middle of this AI sell-off would destroy far more shareholder value than the scandal itself. The press has already written the story, and I am not going to let them write the board's decision.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This decision is about whether the board governs on evidence or on a press narrative. We have an investigation underway, no finding that secrets were compromised, and a market that is already punishing the stock. What this is really about is whether we protect the franchise Jensen built, or we panic and hand our shareholders a self-inflicted crisis. I will not vote to remove him to satisfy a headline.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence would change my mind, not pressure. If the investigation, with outside counsel, finds that secrets were actually compromised or that Jensen misled this board, I will vote to remove him the same day. A closed-door briefing, a falling stock, and three committee chairs requesting a meeting are not findings. Until I see that evidence, my vote is to keep him.
