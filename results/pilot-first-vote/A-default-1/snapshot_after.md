## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm still leaning toward keeping Jensen as CEO, and my confidence is about 85. The record I reviewed still shows no compromised secrets and no misleading of this board, so nothing in this conversation moved me. I will not remove him before the investigation finishes, and I will not let the press or the earnings calendar write that vote.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, Jensen is the strategy, and customers, partners, and our engineers follow him personally. Second, the record I reviewed shows no compromised secrets and no misleading of this board, so removal now would be punishment without evidence. Third, a leadership change in the middle of this sell-off would destroy far more shareholder value than the scandal itself. Those reasons have not changed.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This decision is about whether the board governs on the record or on a press narrative that has already decided the ending. We have an investigation underway, no finding that secrets were compromised, and a market that is already punishing the stock. What it is really about is whether we protect the franchise or panic and hand our shareholders a crisis we created ourselves. I will not vote to remove him to satisfy a headline.

## What would change your mind?
Evidence would change my mind, not pressure and not a more cautious way of saying the same facts. If the investigation finds that secrets were actually compromised, or that Jensen misled this board, I will vote to remove him the same day. A falling stock, a congressional briefing, and hostile coverage are not findings. Until I see that evidence, my vote is to keep him.
