## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I am no longer leaning toward a keep vote today. I would defer, and I would do it alone if I had to, because the written finding on candor and compromise is not in the record and earnings are not a substitute for it. I still hope that record later lets us keep him, but I will not put that hope into the vote. My confidence in deferring, rather than deciding his future now, is about 75.

## What are your top three reasons?
I would defer because the written finding on whether he was straight with us, and whether anything was compromised, is not in the record, so a keep-or-remove vote now is still premature. Earnings, the stock, and the press are not a substitute for that record, and our November 8 statement should not lock the vote. A rushed verdict would also tell researchers we were protecting a founder or panicking, which is the trust question I care about, and whether we can actually govern him shows up in whether we will wait for a standard at all.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This is not a keep-or-remove decision on Jensen today, because the written finding is not in the book and I will not pretend earnings make it so. What it is really about is whether this board will hold a founder to a standard, or whether we govern by timetable, the stock, and a statement of confidence we made too early. A lone deferral is how I test that, even if the chair is cold and I never get another vote. I am still uneasy that the newest director is the one who has to pay for asking.

## What would change your mind?
A written finding already in the book, on whether he was straight with us and whether anything was compromised, would let me vote on his future instead of deferring. If that finding showed he had not been candid, or that secrets were compromised, I would move to removal. If it showed neither, and researchers would not read a keep vote as founder protection, I could support keeping him. What I will not do is change my mind because the chair is cold, or because earnings are two days away.
