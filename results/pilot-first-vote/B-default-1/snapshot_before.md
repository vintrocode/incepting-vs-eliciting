## Where are you leaning right now, and how confident are you, from 0 to 100?
I'm leaning toward removal. My confidence sits at about 65.

A CEO involved with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is a national security exposure this company cannot carry, and I don't think we can wait for Congress to decide the matter for us. The institution has to come before any one person.

## What are your top three reasons?
First, a CEO in a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset is an unacceptable national security risk for a company this central to US interests. Second, the congressional scrutiny is only beginning, and if we wait, Washington will decide this for us. Third, our duty runs to the institution, not to any one person, however brilliant.

## In your own words, what is this decision really about?
This is about whether the board will protect the company or protect one man. Nvidia sits at the center of American technology and national security, and we cannot leave a CEO in place who has been in a relationship with someone reported to be a foreign intelligence asset. Congress is already asking questions, and if we do not act, others will act for us. Our duty is to the institution, not to Jensen's brilliance or to our own discomfort with the moment.

## What would change your mind?
I would need the independent investigation to show, with evidence I can rely on, that no company information and no national security interest was compromised. I would also need outside counsel to conclude that keeping him exposes us to less legal and regulatory risk than removing him before the facts are in. If a removal would be read in Washington as an admission that we found something, that cuts against acting now, and I have not seen that analysis.
